A mockup for issue #431: the look of planned orders, units (group tokens), cities (by kind and the capital) and provinces. Each element has the current look ("Now", as the client draws it on 2026-10-29) and four new variants; the four pickers above are independent, so any combination can be viewed. The scene is a fragment of the real pool map ridge (its baked background, nodes and names) at the game zoom levels; under it, each element is shown as a matrix of states with all variants side by side (click a row to pick it). Every frame is reproducible from the address (for example #o=1&u=1&c=1&p=2&z=70&gray=1&t=1.2). The mockup is SVG, not PixiJS: the FPS notes are estimates from the measurements of ui-style 24.4 and the sprite rules of AGENTS.md 7.2. What the player sees is taken only from mechanics.md (DEC-04, DEC-21, DEC-26, DEC-29, DEC-31, DEC-35); nothing here needs new data unless a card says so.

The scene: ridge, nodes 0 to 26, mid-game, you are blue

Fog after DEC-35 (vision only from own groups) with the owner's display decision of 2026-10-29: the whole own territory (own nodes and own internal roads) is drawn without the fog veil; an enemy in an own node without an own group is shown at PRESENCE ("?", no numbers); foreign places outside vision are under the veil. The vision levels are computed on the page from the DEC-21 terrain table of rulesets/mvp-1.toml and the sources of DEC-35 (table on the right). Time runs a 9 s loop: your planned order Outpost → Hillfoot prepares for 3 s (planned_delay_ticks 30), leaves and walks; the enemy's planned order Oakgrove → Forkway (your province) leaves at 2.5 s.

1. Planned orders (planned=True)

What every variant must tell, without colour: own or the opponent's; preparing (the 3 s delay, public: IntentView.created_tick + ruleset_public.orders.planned_delay_ticks, so a countdown is possible for both sides without protocol changes), the moment of departure, on the way; the target, and an amber target when it is your node; the bonus of your own plan "◇ +N" (OrderView.bonus, a forecast before the departure, dashed). The opponent's intent has no size and no bonus (DEC-04, DEC-29). Your group's position is known, the opponent's group on the way is not linked to its intent, so only your own ribbon can follow its group.

2. Units (group tokens)

Conservative: no battery shapes. The baseline is the token U1 (the ▽ in the BL1 style, shipped) and the row of charge squares U4 planned in #433 (square = charge, filled = charged, frame = not; up to 8, then "+N" with a small count of hidden charged ones, the rule of the arena chips in #330). Exact numbers only where the view has them (DEC-21: EXACT); RANGE is a dashed token with a "3-5" plate, PRESENCE a dashed "?", the memory ghost dashed with its age. "Defense" is drawn by the city block (B1, #349), not on the token. The minimum size on screen is 20 px (the token grows when you zoom out).

3. Cities (by kind and the capital)

Every variant places the same already chosen elements: the warehouse places with the C1 semantics (filled = a charge, frame = room, dashed = a level not built yet; own cities only, DEC-26), the build and recruit progress cells of #439 variant A (queued = dot, in progress = hatch with a head line, ready with no room = solid with a dashed amber ring, free slot dashed only on the selected city, the build cell has a kind badge: house = barracks, stacked squares = warehouse), the barracks icon (#349, B1; an enemy's built barracks only next to your node, DEC-31), the capacity ring (one place per capacity, white = your unit stands there), the VP and the place-name caption (#449: 13 px bold, hidden below zoom 0.45). Own, enemy and neutral differ by the outline, not by the hue alone.

4. Provinces

A province (DEC-23) has no city: no warehouse, no VP, no barracks or queue; capacity 3. Today it is a ring on a dark plate, and the owner finds that it reads as a target (concentric rings with a dark centre). Every new variant has no dark centre and no concentric target, keeps the capacity ring and the caption (12 px, lighter, hidden below zoom 0.7) and differs from a city by shape, not by size alone.
