Water

Concept, not code. The owner's feedback of 2026-10-29 on the #429 rivers: the map may have whole cells of sea or lake; rivers and the borders they form must look better and more natural, in the map's style; no sliver of one area may stick into another; ideally the areas of two nodes touch only where a road links them, elsewhere water or a river separates them. This page re-imagines the pool map ridge that way, in the current look: the baked T2 ground, the territory tint and border, the cities, units and ODb planned orders exactly as the client draws them (the drawing code is the one of provinces-defense.html). The rules do not read any of it: the graph, the roads, travel_ticks, the capacities and vision stay as they are; water is a picture.

Water and rivers: rendering variants

The same geography in four looks plus Now, at the zoom chosen above (at least 0.7), on one spot of the map. The geography is fixed in all four: water cells (two seas in the corners, two bays, four lakes), a stream on every border between areas without a road, four long rivers from a spring or a lake to the sea, and a bridge wherever a long river passes a road (always at the road's border point, where the two areas of the road meet). Grayscale is in the header.

Recommendation: W4 Painted, baked into maps/<id>.bg.webp like the ground: it speaks the T2 language (flat tones, details that thin out at an edge), so the water belongs to the map instead of lying on it, and nothing is drawn per frame. Take the bank of W2 if the owner wants a crisper coast at overview; W3 is the grayscale-safest and the fallback if the soft shore reads too weak in a playtest.

The check: no two unconnected areas touch

Computed by this page from the data it draws: the client cells from the node points (voronoi.ts), the baked ground of today with the baker's own noise (_Noise.warp, the seed of ridge), the proposal from the label grid of the generator (4 world units per cell, 4-neighbour contacts).

Moved nodes

Four nodes sit too close to a neighbour they have no road to (131 to 159 units apart), so the stream between them would be cramped against both cities. Moving them 25 to 45 units gives the stream room. The game does not read coordinates (DEC-27, item 1: only the graph and the attributes are mirrored and simulated), but the validator ties the drawn length of a road to its travel_ticks (15 percent, DEC-27, item 4). Every move below keeps every road of the node within that tolerance, so travel_ticks and with it every simulation result stay the same; a move outside it would change travel_ticks, a balance change of the map that needs a tournament.

Data and rules (summary of the DEC-36 proposal)

  • Rules: none change. Water, rivers and bridges are not read by gow_sim, the server, the bots or the protocol. A river is never an obstacle, a bridge is never a rule; the road is the only thing that connects nodes, as now.
  • Authored data: a sidecar maps/src/<id>.geo.json next to the half: water cells (kind sea or lake, a point, a radius), long rivers (a spring point, optionally the water they flow into), optional node moves are made in the half itself. maps/<id>.json, the loader, map_hash and the protocol do not change.
  • Baked data: gow-maps bake computes the areas (nearest site: a node with its road halves up to the border point, or a water cell; borders warped by noise, the warp fades near roads), cuts a river on every border of two areas without a road, routes the long rivers along area borders, then writes the picture maps/<id>.bg.webp and the vector file maps/<id>.geo.json (shared border chains: the area rings, the water, the river lines, the bridges). The client draws the tint, the border lines and the fog veil from that file instead of its own Voronoi cells, so the tint and the ground finally agree.
  • Validator: a new check geography: two areas share land only if a road connects their nodes; every area is one piece and holds its node; a road crosses water only at a bridge; a node keeps a minimum distance from water.
  • Symmetry: the rules stay mirrored (DEC-27, item 1); the water is free, like the coordinates: the two halves may differ in the picture.
